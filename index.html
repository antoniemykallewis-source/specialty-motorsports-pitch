03 / What we found + what we would fix

MAKE IT EASIER
TO CHOOSE YOU.One issue. One clear fix.

Each finding below explains what we saw, why it could get in a customer’s way, and the practical change we would make.

Finding 01 / 06

Several tools compete with the inventory.

What we found

The homepage puts trade-in and buying-power tabs, a payment / trade bar, chat and accessibility controls around the page.

Why this can lose inquiries

A shopper has several things asking for attention before choosing a vehicle or contacting the dealership. Extra decisions can make a simple inquiry harder.

Our fix

Keep the accessibility tools. Simplify the promotional panels with your website provider, DX1, and make “browse inventory” and “contact us” the clearest next steps.

Proof: Observed homepage UI ↗
Finding 02 / 06

Your ad videos stop short of the homepage.

What we found

Active Meta ads already use video. The homepage we inspected has eight image slides and a virtual-tour link, but no on-page video player.

Why this can lose inquiries

Someone arriving from an ad loses the chance to hear the staff, see the showroom in motion and get a quick answer before calling.

Our fix

Use the video foundation you already have. Film staff-led vehicle walkarounds and buying advice for local Facebook and Instagram ads, then add a lightweight, tap-to-play version to the site.

Proof: Homepage media inspection ↗
Finding 03 / 06

A shopper can find two closing times.

What we found

The main site says 6:00 PM. A separate dealer-branded GotGoodCars site with the same address and phone says 5:30 PM. Its ownership and update status still need confirming.

Why this can lose inquiries

Conflicting hours can make someone hesitate about a late visit or call another dealership for certainty.

Our fix

Confirm the correct hours and who manages the second site. Update or retire outdated pages, then align the Google Business Profile and other important listings.

Proof: Secondary dealer site ↗
Finding 04 / 06

The main heading does not explain the dealership.

What we found

The page’s main text heading is a frequently asked questions heading; the vehicle-selling message is mostly inside image graphics.

Why this can lose inquiries

A quick reader should immediately understand what you sell and where you are. Putting that information in ordinary text also makes it easier for search engines and assistive tools to read.

Our fix

Add a clear Sacramento dealership headline and links to the main vehicle categories. Support the important categories with useful local pages and buying-guide blog posts.

Proof: Rendered heading structure ↗
Finding 05 / 06

Check that inquiries are recorded correctly.

What we found

The public page includes a Meta Pixel, which helps record ad-related actions, and two Google Tag Manager containers, which hold tracking tools. Their presence alone does not prove duplicate or missing results.

Why this can lose inquiries

If calls and inquiries are not connected to the right ads, an owner can keep paying for attention without knowing which activity brings serious buyers. That is a check to complete, not a measured loss.

Our fix

Confirm who owns each tool and test the inquiry route with appropriate consent. Report useful calls, texts and vehicle inquiries, rather than judging ads only by clicks.

Proof: Public source inspection ↗
Finding 06 / 06

The mobile test has room to improve.

What we found

Google’s October 20 mobile lab test scored 23/100. The largest visible element appeared after 44.4 seconds in that simulated test. Separately, its 28-day real-user report passed Google’s page-experience checks, with that element appearing in 2.1 seconds.

Why this can lose inquiries

A slow first screen can interrupt an ad click before a shopper reaches inventory. The lab result is a warning to investigate; the passing real-user result means we should not describe every visit as slow.

Our fix

Work with DX1 to reduce heavy images and unnecessary scripts, load secondary widgets later, and repeat the same test. Preserve the real-user performance that is already passing.

Proof: Mobile PageSpeed report ↗
